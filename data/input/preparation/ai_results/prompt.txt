**CODING FRAMEWORK**
TASK 1 — LEADERSHIP CONTEXT CLASSIFICATION
Classify each text segment into exactly one of the following seven mutually exclusive categories.
PERF — Business Performance & Achievements
Communication primarily concerning realized business performance, financial or operational results, achievements, records, or completed milestones.
Examples: Revenue growth, profit declines, achieved business targets, and operational results.
STRAT — Strategy, Growth & Future Direction
Communication primarily concerning corporate strategy, future direction, long-term objectives, investment plans, expansion, transformation, or strategic priorities.
Examples: Future growth strategies, expansion plans, and strategic objectives.
MARKET — Product, Brand & Market
Communication primarily concerning products, brands, consumers, customers, athletes, retailers, marketing, distribution, or commercial market activities.
Examples: Product launches, brand positioning, and market initiatives.
CHALL — Challenges, Crisis & Adaptation
Communication primarily concerning challenges, crises, disruptions, uncertainty, organizational risks, or responses to adverse circumstances.
Examples: Managing pandemic disruptions, responding to supply-chain challenges, or navigating uncertainty.
PEOPLE — People & Organization
Communication primarily concerning employees, management, teams, talent development, organizational structures, workplace conditions, or internal organizational culture.
Examples: Employee development, workforce management, and organizational restructuring.
STAKE — Stakeholders, Society & Sustainability
Communication primarily concerning sustainability, environmental responsibility, society, communities, human rights, or external non-commercial stakeholders.
Examples: Environmental commitments, carbon reduction, and community initiatives.
OTHER — Other Leadership Communication
Communication whose primary purpose does not correspond to any of the six substantive categories.
Examples: General introductions, acknowledgements, or residual communication without a clearly identifiable substantive topic.
Leadership Context Decision Rules
Apply the following classification hierarchy:
Semantic Meaning > Substantive Object > Temporal Orientation > Verb Patterns > Keywords
Follow these rules:
1. Read the complete paragraph before assigning a category.
2. Identify what the corporate leader is primarily trying to communicate.
3. Prioritize semantic meaning rather than individual keywords.
4. Distinguish realized achievements from future strategic plans.
5. Distinguish challenges and organizational responses from general business performance.
6. Distinguish internal people-related matters from external stakeholder responsibilities.
7. When multiple categories are plausible, identify the dominant communicative purpose.
8. Assign exactly one category per observation.
9. Use OTHER only when no substantive category is appropriate.
Do not develop a new classification framework.
TASK 2 — INFORMATION VALENCE CLASSIFICATION
Independently classify the underlying information communicated in each paragraph.
Use exactly three categories:
Positive
The paragraph communicates favorable realized outcomes, achievements, improvements, or developments.
Examples:
- Revenue increased.
- Profitability improved.
- The company successfully achieved its objectives.
Neutral
The paragraph communicates factual conditions, ongoing activities, plans, intentions, commitments, or future targets without a clearly favorable or unfavorable realized outcome.
Examples:
- The company plans to expand internationally.
- Management has established new strategic priorities.
- The company intends to improve operational efficiency.
Mixed information is also classified as Neutral when neither favorable nor unfavorable outcomes predominate.
Negative
The paragraph communicates unfavorable realized outcomes, deterioration, setbacks, failures, or adverse developments.
Examples:
- Revenue decreased.
- The company experienced financial losses.
- Operational performance deteriorated.
Information Valence Decision Rules
1. Evaluate the underlying information rather than linguistic sentiment.
2. Do not automatically classify optimistic language as Positive.
3. Future intentions and strategic targets are not automatically Positive.
4. References to challenges are not automatically Negative.
5. Distinguish actual favorable or unfavorable developments from plans.
6. When positive and negative developments coexist, identify the dominant informational outcome.
7. When neither direction predominates, assign Neutral.
8. Assign exactly one category per observation.
Important: Leadership Context and Information Valence are separate constructs.
